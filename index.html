Chapter by Chapter

The journey we've already taken children on

This isn't a concept. It's a program we've run in past summer batches — with a beginning, middle, and an end that leaves a child holding something real. Here's exactly how it unfolds.

01
Find the story

Storytelling events & the Ikigai technique

We open with live storytelling sessions, then use the Ikigai technique — a structured way of uncovering what a child is naturally good at, what they enjoy, and where that overlaps with something useful. No two children leave this chapter with the same answer.

02
Nurture it

A pet plant, and a nature-led rhythm

Every child is handed a pet plant of their own — a small, living responsibility that teaches patience and care. This sits inside our broader philosophy: outdoor, nature-led experiences are where a child's confidence actually grows, not inside a screen.

03
Build it

Real kidpreneurs, running real things

By the end, children aren't presenting projects — they're running small ventures matched to who they are.

  • One child now runs a working bakery business
  • Another builds flyers and images for real use, using Claude and AI tools
04
Own it

Away from the phone, by choice

The outcome we care about most: children who move away from being passive mobile consumers because they've discovered something better — being a creator, with a plant they've grown and a small venture they can point to and say, "I built this."

Whole-Child Wellness

Storytelling builds the mind. Yoga balances the rest.

Every Kahaaniya batch includes a yoga practice, led by Rupika — because a confident, creative child also needs to be a physically and mentally balanced one.

Rupika S.
Yoga & Wellness Lead
Leads yoga practice across Kahaaniya's children's programs, and has independently trained corporate teams — including a full session run on the Infosys campus.
Yoga with Rupika, website ↗ Infosys corporate session, reel ↗

Yoga isn't an add-on in our program — it's built into the curriculum children go through, right alongside storytelling and the Ikigai work. The idea is simple: a child who is grounded physically finds it easier to be confident creatively.

We've already proven this works outside classrooms too. Rupika has led corporate yoga training for working teams, including a well-received session at Infosys — the same discipline we'd bring to your staff.

For your school, this means yoga can show up in three places, depending on what you'd like: as part of the children's program curriculum, as a standalone wellness offering for teachers, or as a session for your management team.
